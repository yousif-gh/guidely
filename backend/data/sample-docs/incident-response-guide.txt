Incident Response Guide

Purpose
This guide defines how the team detects, responds to, and resolves production incidents, from first alert to postmortem.

Severity Levels
SEV1: full outage or major data exposure, affects all users. SEV2: significant degradation or a broken core feature, affects many users. SEV3: minor bug or degradation with a workaround, affects few users. Severity is set by the first responder and can be escalated as more information comes in.

Detection and Alerting
Alerts fire automatically from monitoring (latency, error rate, uptime checks) and page the on-call engineer via the incident tool. Anyone can also manually declare an incident by posting in #incidents with a brief description.

First Response
The on-call engineer acknowledges the page within 5 minutes for SEV1/SEV2, and 30 minutes for SEV3. They open an incident channel, assign an incident commander, and post an initial status update within 15 minutes of acknowledgment.

Communication
Status updates are posted every 30 minutes during a SEV1, every hour during a SEV2, until resolved or downgraded. Customer-facing status page updates are handled by the incident commander, not individual engineers.

Resolution
An incident is marked resolved when the underlying issue is fixed or mitigated and metrics return to normal for at least 15 minutes. A rollback is preferred over a forward-fix during active SEV1 incidents unless a fix is trivial and verified.

Postmortem
A blameless postmortem is required for every SEV1 and SEV2 incident, published within 5 business days of resolution. It must include a timeline, root cause, impact, and at least one concrete follow-up action with an owner and due date.

Escalation
If an incident is unresolved after 1 hour (SEV1) or 4 hours (SEV2), the incident commander escalates to the engineering manager on call.
